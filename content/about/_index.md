The general objective of the FLARE project is to anticipate the potential increase in catastrophic wildfires and develop a science-based national strategy for risk mitigation and adaptation in Spain. To achieve this goal, we aim to identify high-priority management areas, with a particular focus on densely populated, fire-prone locations that are likely to experience a significant rise in extreme fires due to changing climate. This information is critical for the development of short-term accelerated management strategies aimed at addressing the ongoing wildfire crisis. The research project is structured around four main objectives, each corresponding to a chapter of the PhD thesis, and three specific objectives. The research conducted to achieve these objectives will result in peer-reviewed papers, highlighting the novelty of these topics relative to previous works in Europe, with a particular focus on Spain. These objectives are as follows:

- Objective 1 (O1). Mapping the wildland-urban interface and predicting its future expansion. This objective involves creating a WUI map for current conditions and identifying spatial patterns of major urbanization in various fire-prone areas.

- Objective 2 (O2). Predicting future burned area trends under climate change projections. The aim here is to estimate the potential change in burned areas across different pyroregions under various climate change scenarios, particularly focusing on extreme fire-weather conditions.
- Objective 3 (O3). Estimating the probability of housing loss during extreme fires. This objective entails developing models to estimate the probability of housing loss during wildfires in Spain. These models will help predict conditional losses in the event of large fires.
- Objective 4 (O4). Assessing the impact of increasing weather extremes and urbanization on the probability of catastrophic fires. This objective involves estimating the expected increase in losses within human communities due to rising weather extremes and urbanization. The results will include maps pinpointing areas most susceptible to significant increases in risk.
